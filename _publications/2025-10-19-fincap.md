**The question.** Short financial videos carry much of their message visually: charts, on-screen tickers, gestures, emotion. When a multimodal LLM captions one, which inputs actually help, and does adding more of them always help?

## What we built

- **Data.** 624 short-form financial YouTube videos (under five minutes, drawn from VideoConviction), with human labels for the recommended ticker and action.
- **Inputs.** Every non-empty combination of transcript (T), audio (A) and video (V): seven settings.
- **Topics.** Captions targeted at five topics: main recommendation, sentiment, video purpose, visual analysis and financial entities.
- **Models and scoring.** Gemini 2.0 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, GPT-4o mini and GPT-5 mini (the GPT models take no audio). Captions are scored by G-VEval, a GPT-4o judge with chain-of-thought reasoning; ticker and action extraction by F1.

## Results

- **Video alone is the best single input on 4 of 5 topics:** 92% on visual analysis and 87% on financial entities (GPT-5 mini), 89% on video purpose (Gemini 2.5 Flash), and 82% on sentiment (GPT-5 mini).
- **Some content only video can see.** On visual analysis, transcripts alone score 14–51% across models; video alone scores 80–92%.
- **More modalities is not always better.** Pairs such as audio + video or transcript + video often beat all three together. The best main-recommendation score (85%) and the best ticker–action F1 (0.60) both come from Gemini 2.0 Flash on audio + video.
- **The right input depends on the question.** For extracting the exact ticker and action, video alone is the weakest input for every model.
